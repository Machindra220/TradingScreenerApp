1. EMA vs SMA

EMA — Exponential Moving Average
  • Gives more weight to recent prices
  • Reacts faster to price changes
  • Better for momentum and short-term swing screening
  • Generates more signals — more noise too
  • Preferred for: EMA 10, 20, 50 in trend-following scans
SMA — Simple Moving Average
  • Weights all prices equally
  • Smoother, less sensitive to short-term moves
  • Better for long-term trend identification
  • SMA 200 is widely watched by institutions
  • Preferred for: SMA 100, 200 as trend filters

Neither is universally better. A common approach: use EMA for shorter periods (10, 20, 50) and SMA for longer periods (100, 200).

2. Crossover vs Alignment — Different Signals

CROSSOVER: EMA 10 Crosses Above EMA 20
Previous bar:
EMA10 ≤ EMA20 ← below or equal
Current bar:
EMA10 > EMA20 ← just crossed

A fresh event. Identifies the exact moment of momentum shift. Use the "within N days" lookback to catch recent crossovers.

ALIGNMENT: EMA 10 Is Above EMA 20
Current bar:
EMA10 > EMA20 ← currently true
(may have crossed days/weeks ago)

A current state. The EMA 10 is above EMA 20 right now — regardless of when it crossed. Use for trend-filter screens.

5. Crossover Lookback

The "within N days" setting controls how recently the crossover must have occurred.

Within 1 day

Crossover happened today only. Strictest filter — very few results on any given day.

Within 5 days

Crossover in the last week. Catches fresh momentum shifts — good default for swing screens.

Within 20 days

Crossover in the last month. Broader — more results. Useful for slower-moving MAs like 50/100.
