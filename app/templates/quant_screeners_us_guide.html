1. Hidden Markov Model (HMM) & Regime Detection

What is a "Detected Regime"? US equity markets alternate between two distinct, unobservable states modelled by a 2-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model fitted to daily percentage returns:
  • Regime 0 (Low Volatility / Accumulation): Price consolidates in a narrow range. Returns have low standard deviation and are mean-reverting. Institutional money is being positioned quietly. Common before earnings-driven breakouts.
  • Regime 1 (High Volatility / Momentum Expansion): Institutional participation accelerates volume and ATR, creating directional momentum. This is where S&P 500 leaders make their fastest multi-week moves.
🎯 US Relevance: After Fed decision days, earnings beats, or sector rotation events, large-cap names frequently flip from Accumulation → Expansion overnight. The HMM detects this algorithmically before price visually confirms.
Technical Implementation
  • Algorithm: GaussianHMM(n_components=2, covariance_type="diag", n_iter=100) from hmmlearn
  • Expansion state = state with higher return variance (argmax over state variances)
  • Regime flip = previous bar in non-expansion, current bar in expansion
  • Posterior probability threshold: > 70% (Baum-Welch forward-backward algorithm)
  • Minimum history required: 120 daily bars (~6 months)
What is "Posterior Probability"? The mathematical confidence (0–100%) that today's price bar belongs to the Expansion regime based on recent return sequence. >70% = high-conviction entry. <50% = likely chop; no trade.
Execution Note for US Markets:

Since US stocks frequently gap on earnings and macro events, enter regime flip setups only if the gap-up is ≤ 3% from prior close. Larger gaps on the flip day mean you're chasing; wait for the first pullback to EMA20 instead.

2. Chandelier Stop & Multi-Tiered US Exits

Formula (Chuck LeBeau, verified correct):
Chandelier Stop = 52W Highest High − (2.5 × ATR₁₄)
  • ATR (Average True Range 14): max(H−L, |H−prev_close|, |L−prev_close|) rolled 14 bars — standard Wilder formula.
  • 2.5 Multiplier: Calibrated for S&P 500 stocks. Large-caps like AAPL or MSFT have lower ATR relative to price; small-caps need wider stops. The screener uses a uniform 2.5× — adjust per stock's historical ATR-to-price ratio for live trading.
  • One-directional ratchet: The stop only moves up as the stock makes new highs — it never steps down.
US-Specific Exit Tiers:
  1. Initial stop: Place a stop-loss at the Chandelier Stop value shown in the table immediately after entry.
  2. At +1R: Move stop to breakeven. Never let a 1R winner become a loss.
  3. At +2R: Book 50% of position. Let remaining half trail the Chandelier Stop.
  4. At +4R: Trail remaining position with a 10-day low stop (tighter than Chandelier for extended moves).
  5. Regime Flip Exit: If the HMM model detects a flip back to Accumulation (next scan), exit regardless of current P&L.

3. StatArb Residuals (O-U Process) & VOLAR for US Markets

Statistical Arbitrage in the S&P 500: PCA strips away the S&P 500's three dominant systematic factors (broad market beta, growth/value tilt, sector rotation) leaving idiosyncratic residuals. An O-U process fitted to these residuals identifies stocks temporarily dislocated from their factor-implied fair value:
  • s-score ≤ -1.75: Stock is ~1.75 standard deviations below its PCA-adjusted equilibrium — statistically cheap vs its factor basket.
  • Half-Life 2–25 Days: Mean reversion expected within 2–25 sessions. This is the practical S&P 500 window; longer half-lives (>25 days) drift into value territory which requires fundamental catalysts the model doesn't capture.
  • Above EMA200: Structural uptrend filter — prevents bottom-fishing in genuine downtrends where the O-U assumption breaks down.
  • Exit at s-score ≥ 0.0: Statistical equilibrium reached — the mispricing has fully reverted.
US-Specific Note: In the S&P 500, StatArb residuals are typically triggered by sector rotation (e.g. Energy names temporarily lagging Materials during a commodity trade), post-earnings vol crush on adjacent names, or index rebalancing pressure on recently added constituents.
3M VOLAR for US Stocks (formula verified correct):
VOLAR₃M = 3-Month Total Return ÷ σ(Daily Returns)

In US markets, VOLAR is especially useful to separate earnings-driven momentum (messy, high volatility) from genuine institutional accumulation (clean, low daily volatility). A stock with VOLAR > 2.0 is advancing efficiently — this is the Minervini-style "tight price action" expressed as a ratio.

  • VOLAR ≥ 2.0: Excellent quality of advance — institutional buying is orderly
  • VOLAR 1.0–2.0: Acceptable — some volatility but trend intact
  • VOLAR < 1.0: Choppy move — requires wider stops, lower position size

4. US Position Sizing & Risk Rules

Risk per Trade
1% of Capital

Never risk more than 1% of total account on a single setup regardless of conviction level. Position size = (Account × 1%) ÷ (Entry − Stop).

Max Open Exposure
6 Positions Max

Running more than 6 concurrent positions from quantitative screeners dilutes monitoring quality. Focus on the top 3 StatArb + top 2 HMM + top 1 VOLAR.

Sector Concentration
Max 2 per GICS Sector

Never hold more than 2 positions in the same GICS sector simultaneously. Sector events (earnings, regulatory, commodity) can hit multiple names at once.
