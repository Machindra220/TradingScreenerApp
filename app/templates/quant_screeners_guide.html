1. Hidden Markov Model (HMM) & Regime Detection

What is a "Detected Regime"? Financial markets are non-stationary: stock price distributions change depending on whether institutional money is quietly accumulating or aggressively pushing a trend. A Gaussian Hidden Markov Model models price returns as switching between two distinct, unobservable ("hidden") states:
  • Regime 0 (Low Volatility / Accumulation): Price moves in sideways ranges with low standard deviation. Returns are mean-reverting.
  • Regime 1 (High Volatility / Momentum Expansion): Institutional participation expands volume and daily true range, creating directional velocity.
🎯 Which is useful for you? Regime 1 (Expansion) is where swing traders capture rapid multi-day advances without suffering through weeks of dead money.
What is "Posterior Probability"? The Posterior Probability is the mathematical confidence (between 0% and 100%) computed by the Baum-Welch and Viterbi algorithms that today's price bar belongs to the Expansion Regime based on recent price action.
  • > 70% Probability: High-conviction entry signal. The statistical parameters overwhelmingly fit an expansion state.
  • < 50% Probability: High likelihood of chop; no trade.
What is a "Regime Flip"? A Regime Flip occurs when a stock spent the previous session in Regime 0 (Chop/Accumulation) and officially crosses into Regime 1 (Expansion) on today's close. Buying right on the flip allows you to enter at the beginning of a fresh impulse move rather than chasing an extended stock.
Recommended Learning Topics:

To dive deeper into the mathematics, search for "Hidden Markov Models for Algorithmic Trading with hmmlearn" or "Stefan Jansen - Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading (HMMs Chapter)".

2. Chandelier Stop & Multi-Tiered Exits

What is a Chandelier Stop and How Does it Work? Developed by market technician Chuck LeBeau, the Chandelier Stop is an adaptive trailing stop designed to keep you in winning trends while preventing premature stop-outs during ordinary pullbacks.

It gets its name because it hangs down from the ceiling of the price. As the stock achieves new 52-week or cycle highs, the ceiling rises, pulling the stop line up with it. The Chandelier Stop only ratchets higher—it never moves downward.

$$\text{Chandelier Stop} = \text{Highest High Since Entry} - (2.5 \times \text{ATR}_{14})$$
  • ATR (Average True Range 14): Measures the stock's true daily volatility across high, low, and gap closes.
  • 2.5 Multiplier: Gives the stock enough breathing room to absorb daily institutional shakeouts without breaching structural support.
How to Use the Chandelier Stop in Daily Execution:
  1. After Entry: Note the current Chandelier Stop displayed in the screener table. Place this as your initial GTT (Good-Till-Triggered) or mental stop.
  2. During the Trend: If the stock makes a new high tomorrow, re-run the screener or recalculate the stop. Update your stop level upward to lock in unrealized profits.
  3. The Exit Trigger: If a daily candle closes below the Chandelier line, close the entire position at market open or on the daily close. Do not hold through a breakdown.

3. StatArb Residuals (O-U Process) & VOLAR Metrics

Statistical Arbitrage & the s-score: Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), we strip away broad market movements (Nifty factor, sector beta) to isolate the idiosyncratic return of the single stock. We then fit an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O-U) stochastic mean-reverting process to these residuals:
  • s-score ≤ -1.75: The stock is statistically oversold relative to its peer group by nearly 2 standard deviations.
  • Half-Life (2 to 25 Days): The estimated duration in days it will take for the mispricing to revert half the distance to its fair-value equilibrium.
  • Exit Target: Exit when the s-score climbs back to 0.0 (statistical equilibrium reached).
3M VOLAR (Volume / Volatility-Adjusted Return): $$\text{VOLAR}_{3M} = \frac{\text{3-Month Total Return}}{\sigma(\text{Daily Returns})}$$ A stock up 20% with calm, orderly daily candles has a much higher VOLAR than a stock up 20% on violent whipsaws. High VOLAR signifies clean institutional accumulation without retail erratic behavior.
